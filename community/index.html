I’m Not a Failure, I’m Human

I AM A FAILURE!

Hi everyone! This post is all about me. Currently, I am facing a dilemma: am I a failure, or am I just human?

Yesterday, a big tech company, Accenture, visited my university for an on-campus recruitment drive. There were 192 eligible students, including me. Out of these 192 students, approximately 100 were eliminated in the first round, which was the technical round.

The second round, the coding round, began in less than five minutes. We were given three questions: one on DSA, one on SQL, and one on DOM manipulation. We had just one hour to solve them all.

It was my first time appearing for a coding round during an on-campus recruitment drive. I started with the SQL question, which included 12 table schemas. I understood the expected output, yet I fumbled badly.

I don't know why I got the idea of creating tables when the question never asked me to do so. It even occurred to me that I should create just one table and check whether the compiler would allow it. Another thought that crossed my mind was to write the SELECT query first and see whether I could get the expected output.

But I don't know what got into my head. I ended up writing eight CREATE TABLE queries before checking whether they would run. Unfortunately, the compiler flagged them as queries that could not be executed. At that moment, I realised that all I had to do was write the SELECT query.

I did manage to complete all three sections within the allotted hour, but the experience was a disaster because I made errors in each section. Despite knowing the questions well enough to solve them and potentially advance to the next round, I couldn't make it because of my own mistakes.

Only six students managed to advance to the next round. Everyone else, including me, was eliminated.

Ever since then, I have been feeling demoralised and constantly questioning myself. I have been thinking of myself as a failure.

Tonight, a wise woman asked me a question:

"Are you a failure, or are you just human?"

That question made me realise something important:

I AM NOT A FAILURE. I AM JUST HUMAN.

Humans make mistakes. They learn, grow, and strive to become better than they were yesterday. It is okay to fail, but it is never okay to give up.

I may have lost this opportunity, but I refuse to let one bad experience define my abilities or my future.

I have now restarted my off-campus job search, applying to various companies for technical roles in Full-Stack Web Development and Database Administration.

This experience has taught me that knowing the answers is not always enough. Staying calm under pressure, managing time effectively, and trusting your own knowledge matter just as much.

One failed opportunity does not make me a failure. It makes me someone who still has more to learn, more to experience, and more to achieve.

And to anyone going through something similar, remember this: you are allowed to feel disappointed. You are allowed to question yourself. But don't let one bad day convince you that you are not capable of achieving great things.

We fall, we learn, and we rise again!

I Drew Gear 5 Luffy With Just a Pencil

1/3

This month Codédex asked us to put the code editor down and write something. I’m a dev, so my first thought was, “What do I even write about?” Then I looked at the sketch sitting on my desk and figured I’d just talk about that.

It’s Luffy from One Piece. Gear 5 Luffy, to be exact. If you haven’t watched it, Luffy is a guy with a straw hat who wants to become the Pirate King, and he’s pretty sure he’ll do it. That’s it. That’s the whole plan.

I picked Gear 5 because of the face. Big eyes, huge grin, hair going everywhere. I had never drawn a One Piece character before, so I wasn’t sure I could pull it off. I figured the worst case was a bad sketch and a wasted evening.

It took me about 4 to 6 hours. I used a pencil and plain paper, and I haven’t done the shading yet. The hair was the worst part. Every curl looks random, but if one is off, the whole thing looks wrong. I erased it multiple times. The face was honestly easier than all the swirly lines around it. I kept thinking I was almost done, and then I’d notice another section I hadn’t touched.

Funny thing is, drawing this reminded me of debugging. You fix one little thing, then notice three more things that need fixing.

I’m trying to get into AI/ML and build my own stuff, and Luffy’s attitude kind of stuck with me. I guess that’s something I can learn from him. You don’t always need everything figured out before starting.

The sketch isn’t perfect. I’ll probably add shading later. But it’s done, and I’m happy with it. Now back to the code editor.
